## About Us

The project was started by one family, but over the last 11 years the land title has been moved into
a foundation and our economic activities are now held in an association, with the entire project now
being run by the community residents as a sociocracy.

Rooted in Buddhist principles but open to every path, we're building a future together based in a
collective responsibility for the land and a commitment to our individual personal development,
spirituality, and peace.

## Our Mission

We are exploring the question "How can humans live together in peace and balance with themselves and
nature?"

Part of this guides us towards healing the ecosystems that surround us, and the other part asks us to
look within.

The association we created has the following objectives to help us move in these directions:

- Organising workshops, courses and environmental training events
- Developing projects for energy and food self-sufficiency
- Promoting the protection of biodiversity and natural heritage
- Promoting the well-being and growth of our residents

We're open to new connections and guidance for the above, so please reach out if you are interested to
partner or support our causes.

## What Life Here Looks Like

We are well established in terms of our infrastructure, our community relationships and our connection
with the wider community in our local valley.

Recently we started using a Roadmap and Task Dashboard to provide more structure and clarity for the
community, helping us work together more effectively as a team to achieve our goals.

Every Friday we host a personal check-in, to help everyone stay connected with one another through a
listen-only style of sharing.

We also share meals together from Mondays - Fridays, and we often share the hosting of a Tuesday
meditation session at 8am, with the option for anyone to host their own weekly session.

We have also hosted yoga, music jam sessions, board games and movie nights.

We go to parties, local festivals and we have a local horse riding instructor in our village.

We're 15 minutes drive from a town and we offer two cars for rent to our community.

We consider ourselves spiritual, because in the quieter moments we hold space for the deeper questions
that guide us through life. We don't pretend to have all the answers, or take ourselves too seriously.

We are living with dogs, cats and chickens.

We mainly speak English and Dutch, with Portuguese and French speakers and learners.
